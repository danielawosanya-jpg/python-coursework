# 30 Organic Posts
### One a day for six weeks. No ad spend, no production budget.

Each entry is a complete post. Shoot short-form video on a phone; the text
versions work as-is on LinkedIn, Facebook and Nextdoor.

**The rule for all thirty:** teach the whole thing. Never withhold the answer
to drive a click. The tool link goes in your bio and at the end of the caption,
not in the middle of the value.

**Standing caption footer** (adapt to your state and license):

> Educational only, not advice or an offer of coverage. [Your Name], [State]
> license #[0000000]. Find your own gaps: [link in bio]

## Week 1 — Auto (the fastest "go look right now" hooks)

**1. The 25/50 wake-up**
> Pull up your auto policy. Find "bodily injury liability." If it starts with
> 25,000 — that is the total available for one person you injure. One ambulance
> ride and two nights in a hospital passes it. Everything above it comes from
> you. Moving to 100,000 is usually under $15 a month.

**2. What the three numbers mean**
> 100/300/100. First number: most paid for one injured person. Second: most for
> everyone in the accident. Third: property damage — the other car. That's it.
> Now you can read your own policy.

**3. Uninsured motorist is the one people drop**
> One in eight drivers has no insurance. Uninsured motorist coverage is the
> only thing that pays when one of them hits you. It's usually the cheapest
> line on the policy, and it's the first thing people cut.

**4. Your deductible is not your problem**
> People obsess over a $500 vs. $1,000 deductible and ignore a liability limit
> that's off by $400,000. One is a bad weekend. The other is a lien on your
> house.

**5. Rental car coverage you already have**
> Before you buy the counter's insurance: check your auto policy's liability
> and comprehensive, and check whether your credit card includes collision
> damage. Most people buy coverage twice.

## Week 2 — Life

**6. The DIME method in 30 seconds**
> Debt. Income times ten. Mortgage. Education. Add them, subtract savings.
> That's roughly how much life insurance you need. Most people are carrying
> less than half.

**7. Your work policy isn't a plan**
> Employer life insurance is usually one or two times salary and it ends the
> day the job does. It's a nice benefit. It is not your coverage.

**8. Term isn't wasted money**
> "I don't want to pay for something I might not use" — you say this about the
> fire insurance on the house you love. Term is cheap precisely because you'll
> probably outlive it. That's the good outcome.

**9. The stay-at-home parent gap**
> If the parent who isn't earning a paycheck dies, someone still has to do
> childcare, transport, meals, household management. Price that out. Then
> notice that person often has no life insurance at all.

**10. Buy it while you're boring**
> Life insurance is priced on your health today. Every year you wait costs
> more, and one diagnosis can close the door entirely. The cheapest policy
> you'll ever be offered is the one available this morning.

---

## Week 3 — Home & property

**11. Market value is not rebuild cost**
> Your home's price includes the land. Insurance rebuilds the structure. After
> the last few years of material and labor costs, a lot of policies are
> covering 2019 construction prices for a 2026 rebuild.

**12. The 80% rule that quietly cuts your check**
> Many policies reduce a *partial* loss payout if your dwelling limit is below
> about 80% of replacement cost. You don't have to burn to the ground to get
> hurt by under-insurance. A kitchen fire is enough.

**13. Flood is never included**
> Not in homeowners. Not in renters. Not anywhere except a separate flood
> policy. Over 20% of flood claims come from outside high-risk zones.

**14. Photograph your house tonight**
> Open every closet and drawer, video the whole thing, narrate what things
> cost, save it off your phone. That ten-minute video is the difference between
> a contents claim you can prove and one you argue about for months.

**15. Renters: it's the liability, not the couch**
> Everyone thinks renters insurance is for stolen laptops. The part that
> matters is personal liability — if someone is injured in your apartment, or
> your bathtub floods three units. Fifteen dollars a month.

## Week 4 — Income & liability

**16. You insured the car, not the engine**
> You insure the car that gets you to work and the house that work pays for.
> The paycheck underneath both is usually uninsured. Disability is more likely
> than death during your working years.

**17. Group LTD has fine print**
> Employer disability usually caps at 60%, caps at a dollar ceiling, and is
> taxable when the premium was pre-tax. The number you'd actually receive is
> often near 40% of your income.

**18. Umbrella is the best value in insurance**
> A million dollars of liability on top of your auto and home, typically
> $150–300 a year. Nothing else on the market gives you that much protection
> per dollar.

**19. Who actually needs an umbrella**
> Own a home. Have savings or investments. Have a teen driver, a pool, a dog,
> a rental property, or a job with a public profile. Two or more of those and
> the math is easy.

**20. They don't sue the policy, they sue you**
> A judgment doesn't stop at your limit. It follows income and assets, and in
> many states it can be renewed for decades. Limits are the whole point.

## Week 5 — Life events (highest intent moments)

**21. New baby**
> Add the child to health coverage within 30 days — that window is real. Then
> revisit life coverage, name a guardian, and check your beneficiaries. Half of
> people never update the beneficiary from before they were married.

**22. Just bought a house**
> The mortgage requires dwelling coverage. It doesn't require enough liability,
> doesn't require flood, and doesn't care that you now have a $400,000 debt
> your life insurance doesn't cover.

**23. Got married**
> Bundle the auto policies — carriers price households, not people. Then update
> beneficiaries on every policy and every retirement account. That's the step
> everyone skips.

**24. Kid started driving**
> Your liability exposure just went up more than any other event in your adult
> life. This is the moment for an umbrella, not the moment to shop on price.

**25. Started a business**
> Your personal policies exclude business activity. Homeowners won't cover the
> inventory in your garage, and personal auto may deny a claim during a
> delivery.

---

## Week 6 — Money & process

**26. Bundle, then verify**
> Bundling usually wins, but not always, and never assume. Get the bundled
> number and the two separate numbers, and compare identical limits. Different
> limits means you're not comparing anything.

**27. Raise deductibles only after the fund exists**
> Going from $500 to $1,000 can free $15–40 a month. Do it only if you can
> write that check today without a card. Otherwise you saved premium and bought
> a problem.

**28. Shop every two years, not every year**
> Carriers reward tenure and then quietly stop. Two years is the sweet spot.
> Shopping constantly costs you loyalty credits for pennies.

**29. Read the declarations page once a year**
> One page, about forty numbers, ten minutes. Put it on the calendar for the
> same day every January. It's the single highest-value financial habit almost
> nobody has.

**30. The best agent tells you to change nothing**
> If every review ends with a recommendation to buy something, that's not a
> review. Most of mine end with "you're fine, come back in two years."

## How to use this bank

- **One post a day, weekdays only.** Consistency beats volume.
- **Same video, four platforms.** TikTok, Reels, Shorts, LinkedIn. Repost, don't
  re-shoot.
- **Reply to every comment for the first hour.** Reach follows engagement, and
  the comments become your next twenty posts.
- **Track what lands.** When one post outperforms, that topic becomes a spoke
  article (see `ORGANIC-PLAYBOOK.md`, Channel 1).
- **Tag your bio link** as `?src=tiktok`, `?src=linkedin` and so on, so the
  admin dashboard tells you which platform actually produces leads rather than
  which produces views.
